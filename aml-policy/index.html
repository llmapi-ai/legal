1. Purpose and Scope

LLM API Inc. (“LLM API,” “the Company,” “we,” “our,” or “us”) is a corporation incorporated under the laws of the State of Delaware, United States. The Company operates an artificial-intelligence inference platform that provides business customers with unified API access to large language models and related AI services, sourced from multiple upstream model providers and the Company’s own infrastructure. Customers purchase prepaid usage and consumption-based access to these services.

LLM API is a technology company. It is not a bank, money services business, money transmitter, payment institution, electronic money institution, virtual asset service provider, or other financial institution. It does not take deposits, does not hold or transmit funds on behalf of customers as a payment service, does not exchange or custody virtual assets for third parties, and does not provide banking or payment services. Accordingly, LLM API is not itself subject to the program requirements that the U.S. Bank Secrecy Act imposes on financial institutions.

Nonetheless, LLM API accepts payment for its services from a global customer base through several payment channels and works with commercial, banking, payment and reseller partners. This exposes the Company to money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, bribery and corruption, and sanctions risks. As a U.S. person, LLM API and its personnel are directly subject to the economic sanctions administered by the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control (“OFAC”) and to applicable export-control and anti-bribery laws, regardless of the Company’s regulatory classification.

This Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Policy (the “Policy”) establishes LLM API’s voluntary, risk-based AML/CTF and sanctions compliance program. The program is designed to be consistent with the recommendations of the Financial Action Task Force (“FATF”), the principles of the USA PATRIOT Act, OFAC sanctions requirements, and prevailing industry good practice, and to satisfy the due-diligence expectations of the Company’s banking, payment and commercial partners.

The purpose of this Policy is to:

  • prevent LLM API’s products, services and payment channels from being used to facilitate money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, corruption or sanctions evasion;
  • establish clear standards and controls for customer identification and verification, due diligence, sanctions screening, payment monitoring, suspicious activity escalation and recordkeeping;
  • establish clear accountability for AML/CTF and sanctions functions across all relevant roles within the Company;
  • support the oversight, independent testing and examination readiness of LLM API’s banking, payment and reseller partners; and
  • protect the Company, its personnel and its stakeholders from legal, regulatory, financial and reputational harm.

This Policy applies to all LLM API employees, officers, directors, contractors and third-party service providers involved in the Company’s operations, regardless of role or seniority. It applies to every stage of the customer lifecycle, from initial application through offboarding, and covers all products, services and payment flows offered by the Company. Personnel whose roles involve customer onboarding, identity verification, payment handling, customer support, product development, partnerships or financial-crime escalation are subject to the specific control obligations described in this Policy and in supporting procedures.

2. Business Overview

LLM API provides business customers with programmatic access to large language models and related AI inference services through a single, unified API. The Company aggregates and routes requests across multiple upstream model providers and its own hosted infrastructure, and offers related tooling, orchestration and billing. The service is delivered digitally, worldwide, without any physical branch or in-person channel.

Customers are businesses and professional developers. Access is sold on a prepaid and/or consumption basis: customers fund an account balance or are invoiced for usage, and consume services against that balance. The Company does not offer services intended for personal, family or household purposes, and does not knowingly onboard anonymous or unverifiable customers.

Payment channels

The Company accepts payment through the following channels, each of which carries distinct financial-crime risk characteristics that inform the controls in this Policy:

  • Card payments, processed through a regulated third-party payment processor (Stripe), for account funding and top-ups;
  • Bank transfers and wire payments, typically for larger or invoiced accounts;
  • Stablecoin and other virtual-asset payments (for example, USDT), received through regulated or screened on-ramp and settlement providers; and
  • Reseller and white-label partner settlement, where access to the Company’s services is distributed through partners who settle on an aggregated basis rather than through direct end-customer payment.

Inherent risk characteristics

Consistent with FATF guidance and industry experience, LLM API recognises the following inherent risks, which are assessed in full in the Business-Wide Risk Assessment (Section 5) and which directly inform the design of the customer due diligence, sanctions, monitoring and training frameworks in this Policy:

  • a fully digital, non-face-to-face onboarding channel, which increases reliance on electronic identity verification and automated anomaly detection;
  • a global, cross-border customer base, which raises exposure to higher-risk jurisdictions, politically exposed persons and sanctions nexus;
  • the acceptance of virtual-asset (stablecoin) payments, which carries elevated money-laundering risk and requires wallet screening and blockchain-analytics controls to detect exposure to illicit, mixing or sanctioned addresses;
  • card payments, which carry fraud and chargeback risk and the risk of funding with stolen or compromised instruments; and
  • distribution through resellers and white-label partners, which introduces indirect customer relationships that require partner due diligence and flow-down of AML/CTF and sanctions obligations.

3. Regulatory Framework and Approach

LLM API is not a financial institution and is therefore not subject to the written AML program requirement applicable to banks and money services businesses under the Bank Secrecy Act. The Company nonetheless adopts and maintains a risk-based AML/CTF and sanctions compliance program as a matter of policy, in order to manage its financial-crime risk, to comply with laws that apply to it irrespective of its classification, and to meet the contractual and due-diligence requirements of its partners.

This Policy is designed to be consistent with, and to give effect to the relevant principles of, the following instruments and standards, to the extent they apply to the Company’s activities:

  • the economic and trade sanctions administered by the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC), 31 CFR Chapter V, which bind LLM API as a U.S. person;
  • the FATF 40 Recommendations and associated guidance on a risk-based approach, customer due diligence, beneficial ownership, virtual assets and the financing of terrorism and proliferation;
  • the principles and information-sharing framework of the USA PATRIOT Act of 2001;
  • the U.S. Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2020, as a statement of U.S. policy direction;
  • the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and other applicable anti-bribery and anti-corruption laws;
  • applicable U.S. export-control and dual-use technology restrictions relevant to the provision of AI services; and
  • the AML/CTF, sanctions and due-diligence requirements imposed on LLM API by its banking, payment and reseller partners under their respective agreements.

Where a partner, processor or applicable law imposes a requirement more stringent than the standards set out in this Policy, the more stringent requirement prevails. The Company structures its program around the internationally recognised pillars of an effective AML/CTF framework: documented internal policies, procedures and controls; a designated compliance officer with appropriate authority; ongoing employee training; independent testing; and risk-based customer due diligence.

4. Compliance Program Structure

LLM API’s AML/CTF and sanctions compliance program comprises the following core components, each supported by this Policy and by subordinate procedures:

  • Governance and accountability — clear allocation of responsibility from the Board and Chief Executive Officer through the designated AML Compliance Officer to operational personnel (Section 6);
  • Risk assessment — a documented, periodically refreshed Business-Wide Risk Assessment that identifies and rates the Company’s inherent risks and the controls that mitigate them (Section 5);
  • Customer due diligence — risk-based identification and verification of customers and their beneficial owners, with enhanced measures for higher-risk relationships (Sections 7–11);
  • Sanctions compliance — screening of customers, beneficial owners and relevant counterparties against applicable sanctions lists at onboarding and on an ongoing basis (Section 14);
  • Ongoing and payment monitoring — monitoring of customer relationships and payment activity across all payment channels for unusual or suspicious behaviour (Sections 12–13);
  • Suspicious activity management — identification, internal escalation, investigation and, where appropriate, external reporting of suspicious activity (Section 15);
  • Training and awareness — role-appropriate, recurring training for all relevant personnel (Section 18);
  • Independent testing — periodic independent review of the program’s design and effectiveness (Section 19); and
  • Recordkeeping — retention of customer, due diligence, screening and investigation records for the required period (Section 20).

5. Risk Assessment

LLM API maintains a documented Business-Wide Risk Assessment (“BWRA”) that identifies, analyses and rates the money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud and sanctions risks inherent in its business, and evaluates the design and operating effectiveness of the controls that mitigate those risks. The BWRA is the foundation of the Company’s risk-based approach: it determines the intensity of due diligence, screening and monitoring applied across the customer base.

Risk factors assessed

The BWRA assesses, at a minimum, the following risk categories:

  • Customer risk — the nature, ownership transparency, industry and behaviour of customers, including the presence of complex ownership structures, shell characteristics, or politically exposed persons;
  • Geographic risk — the jurisdictions of the customer, its beneficial owners and its payment sources, with particular attention to FATF-listed and otherwise higher-risk or sanctioned jurisdictions (Appendix B);
  • Product and service risk — features of the service that could be exploited, including rapid scaling of usage and the resale of access to third parties;
  • Payment-channel risk — the distinct risks of card, bank transfer, virtual-asset and partner-settlement channels, including the elevated risk of stablecoin funding; and
  • Delivery-channel risk — the fully remote, non-face-to-face nature of onboarding and service delivery.

Each customer is assigned a risk rating (for example, standard, elevated or high) derived from these factors. The rating drives the level of due diligence at onboarding, the frequency of periodic review, and the sensitivity of ongoing monitoring. The BWRA is reviewed and updated at least annually, and whenever a material change occurs in the Company’s products, payment channels, customer base, partner arrangements, or the external risk and regulatory environment.

6. Roles and Responsibilities

Effective AML/CTF and sanctions compliance is a shared responsibility. Accountability is allocated as follows:

  • The Board of Directors and Chief Executive Officer hold ultimate accountability for the Company’s compliance culture, approve this Policy, ensure the AML Compliance Officer has sufficient authority, independence and resources, and receive periodic reporting on the program’s status.
  • The AML Compliance Officer (“AMLCO”) has day-to-day responsibility for the design, implementation, maintenance and oversight of the program. The AMLCO owns this Policy and its procedures, maintains the risk assessment, oversees customer due diligence and screening, is the escalation point for suspicious activity, determines whether external reporting is required, delivers training, and reports to senior management and the Board.
  • Operational teams (onboarding, finance and payments, customer support, partnerships and engineering) execute the controls relevant to their functions, apply due diligence and screening, monitor activity, and escalate concerns promptly to the AMLCO.
  • All personnel are responsible for understanding and complying with this Policy, completing required training, remaining alert to red flags, and escalating any suspicion of financial crime without delay. No employee may “tip off” a customer that they are, or may be, the subject of an investigation or report.

7. Customer Identification and Verification (KYC/KYB)

LLM API operates a Know-Your-Customer / Know-Your-Business (“KYC/KYB”) program and verifies the identity of its customers before, or promptly after, establishing a business relationship and providing services, on a risk-sensitive basis. The Company does not establish or maintain relationships with anonymous customers or customers using obviously fictitious identities.

Information collected

For business (legal-entity) customers, the Company collects and records, at minimum:

  • the full legal name of the entity, its legal form, and its jurisdiction and date of incorporation or registration;
  • its registration or company number and registered and principal business address;
  • the nature of its business and intended use of the Company’s services;
  • the identity of its beneficial owners and controllers (see Section 11) and of the individual(s) authorised to act on its behalf; and
  • relevant contact and payment information, including the source of funds used to pay for services.

Verification

Identity information is verified using reliable, independent sources, which may include corporate registries, electronic identity-verification and business-verification tools, documentary evidence (such as certificates of incorporation, registers of directors and shareholders, and government-issued identification of authorised representatives and beneficial owners), and reputable third-party data providers. The Company applies non-documentary and electronic verification methods appropriate to a remote onboarding channel, and escalates to enhanced or documentary verification where risk warrants. Where identity or beneficial ownership cannot be satisfactorily verified, the relationship is not established or is terminated, and the circumstances are escalated to the AMLCO for suspicious-activity consideration.

8. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

Customer Due Diligence is applied on a risk-based basis to every customer relationship. CDD comprises identifying and verifying the customer and its beneficial owners, understanding the nature and intended purpose of the relationship, assigning a risk rating, screening against sanctions and other watchlists, and conducting ongoing monitoring proportionate to the assessed risk.

Standard CDD applies to customers assessed as presenting ordinary risk. The Company also applies, where appropriate and consistent with its risk appetite, simplified measures for clearly low-risk, well-understood relationships, and enhanced measures (Section 9) for higher-risk relationships. CDD information is refreshed through periodic review, the frequency of which is determined by the customer’s risk rating, and on a trigger basis when material changes or red flags arise.

9. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

Enhanced Due Diligence is applied to relationships that present a higher risk of money laundering, terrorist financing or sanctions exposure. Circumstances that trigger EDD include, without limitation:

  • customers, beneficial owners or payment sources connected to higher-risk or FATF-listed jurisdictions;
  • customers that are, or are associated with, politically exposed persons (Section 10);
  • customers with opaque, complex or unusual ownership or control structures;
  • relationships funded substantially through virtual assets, or exhibiting indicators of elevated money-laundering risk;
  • unusually high usage or payment volumes relative to the customer’s stated profile; and
  • resellers and intermediaries that distribute the Company’s services to their own customers.

EDD measures may include obtaining additional identity, ownership and background information; establishing the source of funds and, where relevant, source of wealth; obtaining senior-management approval to establish or continue the relationship; applying more frequent review and more intensive ongoing monitoring; and imposing limits or additional controls on payment channels. The outcome of EDD is documented and retained.

10. Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)

A politically exposed person is an individual who is or has been entrusted with a prominent public function, together with their family members and close associates. PEP status does not of itself imply wrongdoing, but it is a recognised higher-risk indicator requiring additional scrutiny.

The Company screens customers and their beneficial owners for PEP status at onboarding and on an ongoing basis. Where a customer or beneficial owner is identified as a PEP, the Company applies Enhanced Due Diligence, obtains senior-management approval to establish or continue the relationship, takes reasonable measures to establish the source of funds, and applies enhanced ongoing monitoring.

11. Beneficial Ownership

For every legal-entity customer, the Company identifies and takes reasonable measures to verify the identity of each individual who ultimately owns or controls the entity. Consistent with international standards, the Company applies a beneficial-ownership threshold (for example, individuals owning or controlling 25% or more of the entity) together with a control test to capture individuals who exercise control through other means, and in all cases identifies at least one natural person who exercises senior managing control.

Where ownership is layered through intermediate entities, the Company looks through the structure to the ultimate natural-person beneficial owners. Customers that are unable or unwilling to disclose their beneficial ownership are not onboarded, and existing relationships are terminated, with escalation to the AMLCO for suspicious-activity consideration.

12. Ongoing Monitoring of Customer Relationships

The Company conducts ongoing monitoring of its customer relationships to ensure that the activity observed remains consistent with its knowledge of the customer, its business and its risk profile, and to detect changes that may warrant re-rating, enhanced due diligence or escalation.

Ongoing monitoring includes periodic review of CDD information at a frequency determined by the customer’s risk rating; event-driven review triggered by material changes (such as changes in ownership, jurisdiction, payment behaviour or sanctions status); and re-screening of customers and beneficial owners against updated sanctions and PEP lists. Findings that indicate elevated risk or suspicion are escalated to the AMLCO.

13. Payment and Transaction Monitoring

LLM API monitors payments received for its services, across all payment channels, to detect activity that is unusual, inconsistent with the customer’s profile, or indicative of money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud or sanctions evasion. Monitoring is risk-based and is tailored to the distinct characteristics of each payment channel:

  • Card payments — monitored for fraud, chargeback and compromised-instrument indicators, using the risk and fraud controls of the Company’s payment processor (including Stripe Radar) together with the Company’s own velocity and anomaly checks;
  • Bank transfers and wires — monitored for unusual patterns, inconsistency with the customer’s profile, third-party or unexpected-origin payments, and structuring, with source-of-funds enquiry where warranted;
  • Stablecoin and virtual-asset payments — subject to wallet screening and blockchain-analytics controls to detect exposure to sanctioned addresses, darknet markets, mixers, scams or other illicit sources, and to establish, on a risk basis, the provenance of funds; and
  • Reseller and partner settlement — reconciled and reviewed at the partner level, supported by the partner due diligence and obligation flow-down described in Section 22.

Indicators and escalation

Monitoring is informed by the red-flag typologies set out in Appendix C. Alerts and anomalies are reviewed and, where they cannot be satisfactorily resolved, escalated to the AMLCO for investigation and suspicious-activity consideration. The Company applies holds, additional verification, or restrictions on payment channels and account activity where appropriate to manage risk during investigation.

14. Sanctions Compliance

As a U.S. person, LLM API is bound by OFAC-administered sanctions and does not provide services to, or process payments from or for, individuals, entities or jurisdictions that are the target of applicable sanctions. The Company also has regard to the sanctions regimes of the United Nations, the European Union and the United Kingdom (HM Treasury / OFSI) where relevant to its customers and counterparties.

Screening

  • The Company screens customers, their beneficial owners and, where relevant, counterparties against applicable sanctions lists (including the OFAC Specially Designated Nationals and Consolidated lists and relevant UN, EU and UK lists) at onboarding and on an ongoing basis, including re-screening when lists are updated;
  • the Company applies geolocation and jurisdiction controls to restrict access from comprehensively sanctioned territories;
  • virtual-asset payments are screened at the wallet level against sanctioned-address data; and
  • potential matches are investigated and, where confirmed, the Company blocks or rejects the transaction or relationship as required, freezes assets where legally obligated, and makes any report required to the competent authority.

A confirmed sanctions match is escalated immediately to the AMLCO, who determines the required action and reporting. The Company does not engage in conduct designed to evade or circumvent sanctions and prohibits facilitation of sanctioned activity by any third party.

15. Suspicious Activity Identification and Escalation

All personnel must remain alert to indicators of money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud, sanctions evasion or other financial crime, and must escalate any knowledge or suspicion promptly to the AMLCO through the internal escalation channel. Escalations must be made regardless of the amount involved and even where the activity has not completed.

The AMLCO investigates each escalation, documents the analysis and the decision reached, and determines the appropriate action. Because LLM API is not a financial institution with direct suspicious-activity-report filing obligations under the Bank Secrecy Act, suspicious activity is managed by: (i) documenting and retaining the internal investigation; (ii) notifying and cooperating with the Company’s relevant banking, payment or processing partner, in accordance with the applicable agreement, so that the partner may discharge any regulatory reporting obligation; and (iii) making any report that LLM API is itself required by applicable law to make to a competent authority. Where appropriate, the Company restricts, suspends or terminates the relationship. Confidentiality is maintained and tipping-off is prohibited.

16. Anti-Bribery and Corruption

LLM API prohibits bribery and corruption in all forms, whether involving public officials or private parties, and whether offered, promised, given, requested or received, directly or through third parties. The Company complies with the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and other applicable anti-bribery laws. Facilitation payments are prohibited. Personnel must exercise care with gifts, hospitality and the engagement of intermediaries, and must escalate any bribery or corruption concern to the AMLCO. Bribery and corruption risk is considered within the Company’s due-diligence and partner-oversight processes.

17. Prohibited Customers and Uses

The Company will not knowingly establish or maintain a relationship with, and will act to terminate, any of the following:

  • individuals, entities or jurisdictions targeted by applicable sanctions, and persons acting on their behalf;
  • customers who refuse to provide, or who provide false or misleading, identity or beneficial-ownership information;
  • shell entities with no apparent legitimate business purpose, and entities whose ownership or control cannot be established;
  • customers reasonably believed to be using, or intending to use, the Company’s services or payment channels for money laundering, terrorist or proliferation financing, fraud, sanctions evasion or other illegal purposes; and
  • customers whose use of the services breaches the Company’s Acceptable Use Policy in a manner connected to financial crime.

18. Training and Awareness

LLM API provides AML/CTF and sanctions training to all relevant personnel at onboarding and on a recurring basis (at least annually), with additional role-specific training for personnel in onboarding, finance and payments, support, partnerships and compliance. Training covers the Company’s obligations and this Policy, the recognition of red flags and typologies, sanctions and PEP considerations, virtual-asset risk, and the escalation process, including the prohibition on tipping-off. Completion of training is recorded and monitored, and the AMLCO updates training content to reflect emerging risks and regulatory developments.

19. Independent Testing and Audit

The design and operating effectiveness of the AML/CTF and sanctions program is subject to periodic independent testing, conducted by a party independent of the functions being tested (which may be an internal party with sufficient independence or an external specialist), on a risk-based frequency and at least once every twelve to twenty-four months. Testing covers the adequacy of the risk assessment, policies and procedures, the effectiveness of customer due diligence, screening and monitoring, training, and recordkeeping. Findings are reported to senior management, and remediation is tracked to completion by the AMLCO.

20. Recordkeeping and Retention

The Company creates and retains records sufficient to demonstrate compliance with this Policy, including customer identification and verification records, beneficial-ownership information, risk assessments and ratings, due-diligence and EDD files, sanctions and PEP screening results, payment-monitoring alerts and dispositions, suspicious-activity investigations and decisions, and training records.

Records are retained for at least five years following the end of the customer relationship or the completion of the relevant transaction or investigation, or longer where required by applicable law or by a partner agreement. Records are stored securely, with access restricted to authorised personnel, and are protected in accordance with the Company’s information-security and data-protection policies.

21. Customer Exit Management

Where the Company determines that a relationship presents unacceptable financial-crime risk — including where identity or beneficial ownership cannot be verified, where a sanctions nexus exists, or where suspicion of financial crime arises — it restricts, suspends or terminates the relationship in a controlled manner. Exit decisions are documented and approved by the AMLCO, carried out without tipping off the customer, and accompanied by any legally required reporting and by appropriate retention of records. The Company ensures that exit does not frustrate any legal obligation to freeze assets or to report.

22. Third-Party and Partner Oversight

LLM API relies on third parties — including its payment processor, virtual-asset on-ramp and settlement providers, identity-verification and screening vendors, and reseller and white-label partners — and recognises that these relationships can introduce financial-crime risk. The Company conducts risk-based due diligence on material third parties before engagement and periodically thereafter, and assesses their own AML/CTF and sanctions controls.

For resellers and white-label partners who distribute the Company’s services to their own customers, the Company flows down AML/CTF and sanctions obligations through contract, requires partners to maintain appropriate KYC/KYB and screening over their customers, reserves rights of information, audit and termination, and monitors partner settlement activity. Reliance on a third party for any element of customer due diligence does not relieve LLM API of ultimate responsibility for compliance with this Policy.

23. Governance, Change Management and Policy Review

This Policy is owned by the AML Compliance Officer and approved by the Chief Executive Officer and the Board. Material changes to the Company’s products, payment channels, customer base, partner arrangements or applicable legal and regulatory requirements are assessed for their impact on financial-crime risk, and this Policy and its supporting procedures are updated accordingly under the Company’s change-management process.

This Policy is reviewed, and re-approved, at least annually (every twelve months), and more frequently where warranted by a material change in risk. The AMLCO reports periodically to senior management and the Board on the status and effectiveness of the program, significant risks and incidents, and the results of independent testing.

24. Related Documents

This Policy is supported by, and should be read together with, the Company’s subordinate procedures and related policies, which may include: the Customer Onboarding and KYC/KYB Procedure; the Sanctions Screening Procedure; the Payment and Transaction Monitoring Procedure; the Suspicious Activity Escalation Procedure; the Business-Wide Risk Assessment; the Acceptable Use Policy; the Privacy Policy and Data Protection documentation; and partner-specific compliance schedules. In the event of a conflict between this Policy and a mandatory requirement of applicable law or a partner agreement, the more stringent requirement prevails.

A. Appendix A — Key Definitions

  • Beneficial Owner — a natural person who ultimately owns or controls a customer, directly or indirectly, including through ownership at or above the applicable threshold or through other means of control, and in all cases a natural person exercising senior managing control.
  • CDD / EDD — Customer Due Diligence / Enhanced Due Diligence, the risk-based measures applied to identify, verify, understand and monitor a customer.
  • CTF — Counter-Terrorist Financing.
  • FATF — the Financial Action Task Force, the inter-governmental body that sets international AML/CTF standards.
  • KYC / KYB — Know Your Customer / Know Your Business, the process of identifying and verifying customers and the businesses behind them.
  • Money Laundering — the process of concealing the origin, ownership or control of proceeds of crime to make them appear legitimate.
  • OFAC — the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control, which administers U.S. economic and trade sanctions.
  • PEP — Politically Exposed Person, as defined in Section 10.
  • Sanctions — restrictive measures imposed by governments and international bodies against targeted persons, entities, sectors or jurisdictions.
  • Virtual Asset / Stablecoin — a digital representation of value that can be transferred or traded; a stablecoin (such as USDT) is a virtual asset designed to maintain a stable value against a reference asset.

B. Appendix B — Higher-Risk and Monitored Jurisdictions

The Company treats as higher-risk, and subjects to enhanced measures or restriction, customers, beneficial owners and payment sources connected to: jurisdictions subject to comprehensive sanctions; jurisdictions identified by the FATF as “High-Risk Jurisdictions subject to a Call for Action” or as “Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring”; and other jurisdictions assessed by the Company as presenting elevated money-laundering, terrorist-financing, corruption or sanctions-evasion risk. The applicable lists are dynamic; the Company references the current FATF and OFAC publications and updates its controls accordingly as part of ongoing monitoring and the periodic refresh of the Business-Wide Risk Assessment.

C. Appendix C — AML Red Flags and Typologies Reference

The following non-exhaustive indicators may, alone or in combination, warrant escalation to the AMLCO:

  • reluctance or refusal to provide, or provision of false or inconsistent, identity or beneficial-ownership information;
  • ownership or control structures that are unnecessarily complex or opaque, or that appear designed to obscure beneficial ownership;
  • a customer, beneficial owner or payment source connected to a sanctioned or higher-risk jurisdiction without a clear business rationale;
  • payments from third parties unconnected to the customer, or from unexpected or inconsistent origins;
  • virtual-asset payments from wallets with exposure to mixers, darknet markets, scams, or sanctioned or otherwise illicit addresses;
  • card funding exhibiting fraud, chargeback or compromised-instrument indicators, or rapid use of multiple instruments;
  • transaction or usage patterns inconsistent with the customer’s stated profile, including sudden or unexplained changes in volume;
  • structuring of payments to stay below thresholds or to avoid scrutiny;
  • rapid onboarding, funding and consumption followed by a request for refund or redirection of funds; and
  • indications that the customer is reselling or redistributing access to undisclosed or unverifiable third parties.
